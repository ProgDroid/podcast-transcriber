When a scope decision depends on a fact a cheap probe would settle, he takes the probe first. Offered four ways to size a measurement task — three substantive builds and one "run the free listing, then decide" — he chose the listing. It returned a population five times smaller than assumed and disproved the stated reason for the gap. Asked again with real numbers, he immediately chose the heaviest of the remaining options.

**Why:** this is sequencing, not caution. Committing to scope before the population is known sizes the work against a guess, which is the failure his PRE-REGISTER THE NUMBERS discipline exists to prevent. Both choices are the same principle applied twice.

**How to apply:** the standing "lead with rigor — offer the more thorough option first" rule still holds, but only *after* any cheap measurement that would change the thorough option's shape. When a probe costing minutes could resize the work, make it its own option rather than folding it in as step one of a bigger plan, and expect to be asked again once it lands. Corollary: do not treat his picking the cheap option as a preference for less rigour — he spent the saving on more.
